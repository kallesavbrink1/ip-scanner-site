IP Scanner

Find and identify every device on your network

See what is on your network. Scan your Wi-Fi in seconds and get a clear list of every device: its name, what kind of device it is, its address and how fast it answers.

Devices

  • Finds and identifies phones, computers, TVs, speakers, printers, routers and smart-home devices
  • Model, manufacturer and software, when the device reports them
  • MAC address, when the device reports it itself
  • New and offline devices, scan history, your own names and notes
  • Works on iPhone and iPad

Network

  • Your address, router, subnet and DNS servers at a glance
  • Public IP address and country, connection check and speed test
  • Simple security hints, such as open Telnet or FTP
  • Export results as CSV, JSON or PDF

Tools

  • Ping – latency and packet loss
  • Traceroute – path and latency per hop
  • Port Scanner – open TCP ports on a host
  • DNS Lookup – A, AAAA, MX, TXT and other records
  • HTTP Inspector – status, headers, TLS and timing
  • Subnet Calculator – CIDR, masks and host ranges
  • Wake on LAN – wake a sleeping computer

Private by design

No account, no ads and no tracking. Your scan results stay on your device. Use IP Scanner only on networks you own or are allowed to test.

Questions

Does IP Scanner work on mobile data?

No. It scans the Wi-Fi or Ethernet network you are connected to. Mobile data has no local network to scan.

Why does the app ask for access to the local network?

iOS requires your permission before an app can contact devices on your network. Without it, a scan finds nothing. You can change this in Settings › Privacy & Security › Local Network.

Why are some devices unnamed?

A device gets a name only if it reports one on the network. You can rename any device yourself: open it and tap Edit.

Why don't I see every device's MAC address?

iOS does not let apps read MAC addresses directly. The app shows one only when the device reports it itself. Phones often use a private, randomized address for each network.

Is my data sent anywhere?

Scan results, names and notes stay on your device. The app makes only a few internet requests: your public IP address and country, a connection check, and the speed test when you start it. See the privacy policy.

Wake on LAN doesn't wake my computer.

The computer must have Wake on LAN turned on, and it usually needs to be connected by Ethernet to this network. Some routers block the broadcast packet.

A device shows as offline but it is on.

Some devices sleep or ignore network probes. Pull down on the device list to scan again.
